Just completed “Artificial Intelligence: Implications for Business Strategy” at MIT Sloan Executive Education (with CSAIL).

The most useful lesson wasn’t a model or a technique. It was this: in a real business, the constraint on AI is almost never the algorithm — it’s the data foundation and the clarity of the problem you point it at.

I got to test that by building an AI strategy for an actual company over the past weeks. The flashy use cases were tempting. The needle-mover turned out to be unglamorous: clean data, understanding what actually drives a sale, and sequencing a few high-confidence moves before anything fancy.

Two things I’m taking with me:

- Start from the problem, then match the AI to it. Not the other way around.
- The best results come from people and machines each doing what they’re best at.

For the operators and investors I work with, the edge isn’t in having the most models. It’s the discipline to do the boring groundwork first.
