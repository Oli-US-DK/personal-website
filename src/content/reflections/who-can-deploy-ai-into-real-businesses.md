The most important question in enterprise AI right now isn't which model is best. It's who can actually deploy AI into real businesses.

The Economist's Schumpeter column this week put a number on the market's verdict: Accenture's share price is down more than 50% YTD. Investors don't believe traditional consulting will own the AI deployment moment. At the same time, OpenAI and Anthropic both formed joint ventures with major PE houses in May, and OpenAI quietly acquired a British tech consultancy. The model-makers are openly building the deployment muscle they don't yet have.

The piece names Palantir as "closer to what OpenAI and Anthropic might eventually build" — forward-deployed engineers, treating a firm's data as its "ontology," consultants elevated to engineering status. The market hasn't fully bought it either: Palantir's stock has also collapsed in recent months. The reason most observers missed: an FDE-and-ontology-platform is necessary but not sufficient. You also need deep domain expertise at scale to identify which steps to automate, how to design the workflow, and which guardrails matter in each specific context. That's the ingredient even Palantir is missing.

I had the chance to discuss this on Valley101 (~4M subscribers, the Chinese-language tech podcast) with Yiwen Lu, focused on what the OpenAI Deployment Company launch signals. My view: model-makers are doing exactly the right thing. Selling chatbots will not justify the capital flowing into data centers — they have to prove enterprise ROI to keep the funding cycle going. But the GTM motion is fundamentally different from how they've sold so far. A horizontal LLM and a customized workflow rebuild are different products: different selling motions, different talent profiles, different time horizons.

The companies that will define this category combine four things that don't usually sit in the same building: a proprietary data and ontology platform; a model-agnostic orchestration layer that picks the right model for each step of the workflow; forward-deployed engineering capacity embedded inside the customer; and — the ingredient Palantir is missing — a marketplace of tens of thousands of vetted domain experts.
